Zapier invented this category and still owns the mindshare. Make (formerly Integromat) is the tool people move to when the Zapier invoice starts raising eyebrows. Both are hosted, no-code, and genuinely capable. The difference is a trade you make deliberately: Zapier sells you simplicity, Make sells you power for less money. Here's how that plays out.

## The billing models are the real story

**Zapier counts tasks.** A task is roughly one successful action. A Zap that fires 1,000 times with two actions burns ~2,000 tasks. Simple to reason about, and expensive — Zapier's paid tiers escalate quickly, and mid-size automation use routinely lands in the hundreds of dollars per month.

**Make counts operations.** An operation is one module executing. A 10-step scenario running 1,000 times burns ~10,000 operations. Sounds worse — but Make's operations are priced *far* cheaper per unit, and its plans include vastly more of them. In practice, comparable automation runs meaningfully cheaper on Make, frequently by a large multiple.

The honest caveat: Make's meter punishes complexity. A scenario with lots of routers, iterators, and aggregators consumes operations fast. Zapier's punishes volume. Model your actual workflows against both, because the answer genuinely flips depending on shape.

## Where Zapier wins

**The app library.** Around 7,000+ integrations versus Make's ~1,500-2,000. If you use a niche tool, Zapier probably supports it and Make probably doesn't. This is Zapier's moat and it's a real one — nothing else in the article matters if your app isn't there.

**Simplicity.** The linear trigger→action model is genuinely easy. A non-technical person builds their first working Zap in ten minutes with no concept of routers or data structures.

**Reliability and maturity.** It's been running critical business automation for over a decade. Error handling, replays, and history are polished.

**Documentation and community.** Every problem you'll have has a StackOverflow answer or a template.

## Where Make wins

**Cost.** Not marginally — substantially. This is why people migrate.

**Real branching logic.** Routers send data down multiple conditional paths in one scenario. Zapier's paths are more limited and gated to higher tiers. If your automation has "if this, do these three things, otherwise do that," Make expresses it naturally.

**Data handling.** Iterators and aggregators let you loop over arrays and reassemble results — the kind of thing Zapier makes awkward or impossible without a code step.

**The visual canvas.** You see the whole scenario as a flow, not a vertical list. For anything with branches, this is a much better mental model.

**Error handling.** Per-module error routes, retries, and fallbacks are more granular.

## Where each genuinely hurts

**Zapier:** the price, plainly. Multi-step Zaps and the useful features live on higher tiers, and the bill grows faster than your usage feels like it should. Complex logic is a fight.

**Make:** the learning curve is real — routers, iterators, and data mapping require you to think about data structures, which is exactly the thing no-code users came here to avoid. The app library gaps are frustrating. And the operations meter is harder to predict than Zapier's task count.

## The migration reality

People rarely regret moving from Zapier to Make on cost. They do underestimate the effort: scenarios don't port, you rebuild them, and rebuilding a mature Zapier setup is days of work, not an afternoon. Budget for that honestly before you switch to save $40/month.

The other honest note: if your automation is three simple Zaps, **stay on Zapier**. The savings don't justify the learning curve, and Zapier's free and cheapest tiers are perfectly reasonable at that scale.

## The decision, simplified

**Choose Zapier if** you need a niche integration only it supports, you want the easiest possible tool, your automation is simple, or you're at a company where "it just works" beats saving a few hundred a month.

**Choose Make if** your Zapier bill is uncomfortable, your workflows need real branching or data manipulation, you're willing to spend a weekend learning, or you're running enough automation for the cost gap to matter.

## The honest verdict

Make is the better *tool* for anyone willing to learn it, and dramatically better value. Zapier is the better *product* for anyone who wants automation without thinking about automation, and its integration catalogue is unmatched.

The failure mode is picking on price alone and discovering your critical app isn't supported. Check your integration list first — if Make covers every app you actually use, the cost argument is close to decisive. If it doesn't, that's the end of the discussion regardless of price.

Whichever you land on, check for a current promotion before committing; both discount annual billing significantly, and automation subscriptions are the kind you keep for years.
