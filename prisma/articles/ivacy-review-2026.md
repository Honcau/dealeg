Ivacy is one of those VPNs you mostly encounter through a deal. It rarely wins head-to-head reviews, it isn't the fastest, and it doesn't have the brand recognition of the big names — but it's persistently, aggressively cheap on long plans, and it's been operating since 2007. If you've seen it advertised and wondered whether the low price hides something, here's an honest look.

## The thing you should know first

Ivacy and PureVPN are widely reported to share corporate roots — the same parent group behind both brands. Neither hides this especially hard, but neither leads with it either, and it matters for two reasons.

First, if you're diversifying providers for redundancy or trust reasons, picking Ivacy as your "alternative to PureVPN" doesn't diversify much. Second, PureVPN's 2017 incident — where it provided connection logs to the FBI despite no-log marketing — is part of the same corporate story, even though the incident itself involved PureVPN specifically. Ivacy has its own audit history and policies, but if that event is a dealbreaker for you, it's reasonable to apply the same scepticism here.

That's not an accusation. It's context that most reviews leave out.

## What it does well

**Price.** This is the whole pitch, and it's a real one. Ivacy's multi-year deals routinely land among the cheapest in the market — often well under $2/month equivalent when heavily discounted. For a VPN that works, that's genuinely inexpensive.

**Server coverage.** Thousands of servers across a broad country list, comparable to much better-known providers. Finding an exit where you need one is rarely a problem.

**Devices.** Generous simultaneous connections and apps across all the usual platforms plus routers. One subscription covers a family.

**Features exist.** Split tunnelling, port forwarding, dedicated IP, and a kill switch are all available — several as paid add-ons rather than included in the base price.

## What it doesn't do well

**Speed.** Ivacy is adequate rather than fast. For browsing, general privacy, and standard streaming it's fine. If you're moving large files, gaming, or connecting across continents, you'll feel the gap against the top tier.

**Apps feel dated.** They work, but the polish is a step behind NordVPN or Surfshark. Small things — connection reliability on flaky networks, reconnect behaviour — are less refined.

**Streaming is inconsistent.** It unblocks some libraries some of the time. If a specific streaming service is your reason for buying, don't buy Ivacy for that; buy something that treats it as a headline feature.

**Support is mixed.** Chat exists and responds, but depth varies. You'll sometimes get a scripted answer to a technical question.

**The renewal.** As always, the eye-catching monthly figure is a multi-year prepay. Renewal is much higher. This is the single most common way people overpay for VPNs — set a calendar reminder the day you buy.

## Who it's actually for

Ivacy is a reasonable buy for **casual, price-driven privacy**: encrypting public Wi-Fi, keeping your ISP out of your browsing, light geo-shifting, covering a lot of household devices for very little money. On those terms, it does the job and the price is hard to argue with.

It's the wrong choice if speed matters, if reliable streaming is the goal, or if your threat model is serious enough that provider trust is the deciding factor. In that last case, the shared corporate history with PureVPN is a real consideration and you should weigh it deliberately.

## The honest verdict

**Choose Ivacy if** you want a working VPN for the least money, you've read the corporate-history note and are comfortable, and your needs are everyday privacy rather than performance or unblocking.

**Choose something else if** you want speed (NordVPN), the best price-to-quality balance with unlimited devices (Surfshark), or a provider whose trust story has no asterisks at all (Mullvad).

The fair summary: Ivacy is cheap and it works. It isn't hiding anything sinister, but it also isn't a bargain version of a premium VPN — it's a budget VPN priced like one, with the compromises you'd expect. If that's what you want, buy it on a deal, and diarise the renewal.
