GetResponse and Mailchimp are both established email marketing platforms, but they've grown in different directions. Mailchimp is the household name that popularized email marketing for small businesses, now expanded into a broader marketing suite. GetResponse is the feature-rich challenger that bundles email with landing pages, automation, and even webinars. If you're choosing where to build your email list, here's an honest comparison of what each does well and where each falls short.

## What they both do

Both let you build an email list, design campaigns, automate sequences, and track results. Both offer free or low-cost entry tiers, drag-and-drop email builders, and the core tools a small business needs to start email marketing. For basic newsletter sending, either works fine — the differences show up as your needs grow.

## Where GetResponse pulls ahead

GetResponse packs in more for the money. Beyond email, it includes landing page builders, marketing automation with a visual workflow editor, sales funnels, and — uniquely among mainstream email tools — built-in webinar hosting, which is a genuine differentiator if you run online events or want to build a funnel around them. Its automation is powerful and available on relatively affordable tiers, where competitors often gate advanced automation behind pricier plans. For an all-in-one marketing toolkit at a reasonable price, GetResponse offers strong value.

GetResponse's weaknesses: the interface, while capable, can feel busy given how many features it packs in. It's less of a household name, so there are fewer third-party tutorials than for Mailchimp. And some of the bundled features (like webinars) are only valuable if you actually use them — if you just want email, you're paying for breadth you may not need.

## Where Mailchimp pulls ahead

Mailchimp's strengths are polish and ecosystem. Its interface is famously friendly and well-designed, its templates are attractive, and its brand recognition means integrations with virtually every other tool exist, plus abundant tutorials and community help. For a small business that wants a smooth, well-supported experience and mainly needs email with some marketing extras, Mailchimp is approachable and reliable. Its reporting and audience insights are polished.

Mailchimp's weaknesses: it gets expensive as your list grows, and pricing is based on contacts (including, at times, unsubscribed ones unless managed carefully), which can escalate. Advanced automation and some features are gated behind higher tiers, so you can outgrow the affordable plans quickly. Some users have also found its pricing changes over the years frustrating, with features moving between tiers.

## Cost as your list grows

This is where many decisions land. Both are affordable at small list sizes, but costs diverge as you scale. GetResponse often includes more (automation, landing pages, funnels) at a given price point, while Mailchimp's advanced features push you toward pricier tiers. If you'll rely heavily on automation and multiple marketing tools, GetResponse's bundling tends to be more economical; if you want mainly polished email with the widest integrations, Mailchimp's ecosystem has value. Both run promotions, so check for current deals before committing.

## The honest verdict

**Choose GetResponse if** you want an all-in-one marketing platform (email + landing pages + automation + webinars), value getting advanced automation on affordable tiers, or are building funnels and running online events. For feature-per-dollar, it's the stronger package.

**Choose Mailchimp if** you want the most polished, beginner-friendly experience, value the widest integration ecosystem and abundant tutorials, and mainly need email with some marketing extras. For sheer ease and familiarity, it's hard to beat.

The practical question: do you want a broad marketing toolkit or a polished email-first tool with a big ecosystem? If you're going to use automation and funnels seriously, GetResponse gives you more for your money. If you value simplicity and integrations above breadth, Mailchimp delivers. Both let you start free, so test each with a small list before committing — and grab a current promo code before moving to a paid annual plan, since the savings can be real.
