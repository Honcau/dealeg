Moving a WordPress site to managed hosting like Kinsta sounds intimidating — you picture broken pages, lost data, and downtime. In reality, migrating to a premium managed host is usually smoother than staying on struggling cheap hosting, because good managed hosts do the hard part for you. This guide walks through migrating a WordPress site to Kinsta (the principles apply to most premium managed hosts), including the preparation that prevents problems.

## Why migrate at all

If your site is slow, frequently down, or you've outgrown budget shared hosting, moving to managed WordPress hosting buys you speed, automatic backups, staging environments, and expert support. The trade-off is cost — managed hosting is premium — so migrate when performance and reliability genuinely matter for your site, not just because you can.

## Step 1 — Before you touch anything: back up

Never start a migration without a complete, off-site backup of your current site: both the files and the database. Even though the migration shouldn't lose anything, a backup means a mistake is recoverable rather than catastrophic. Use a backup plugin or your current host's export, and store the backup somewhere other than the old server.

## Step 2 — Use the free migration service

This is the key insight: premium managed hosts typically offer free professional migrations, and Kinsta is among them. Rather than moving the site manually, you request a migration and their team handles the technical transfer for you. This is dramatically safer than DIY — they've done thousands of migrations and know the pitfalls. Provide your current host's login details through their secure form, and they move the site to a staging environment on their platform.

If you prefer to migrate yourself, a plugin-based approach (exporting your site and importing it on the new host) works too, but for a site that matters, letting the experts do it free is the smarter choice.

## Step 3 — Test on staging before going live

Once your site is on the new host's staging environment, test it thoroughly *before* pointing your domain there. Click through pages, check forms, test your shopping cart if you have one, and confirm plugins work. Staging lets you catch any issues while your live site is still running untouched on the old host — no visitor sees a problem. This step is why managed hosting migrations are low-risk: nothing goes live until you've verified it.

## Step 4 — Point your domain (the actual switch)

When staging looks right, you make the switch by updating your domain's DNS to point at the new host. This is the moment traffic starts flowing to the new server. Because DNS changes take time to propagate globally (from minutes to a few hours), plan the switch for a low-traffic period, and keep the old host active until propagation completes so no one hits a dead site during the transition.

## Step 5 — After the switch

Once DNS has propagated and traffic is on the new host, confirm everything works on the live site, set up SSL (managed hosts usually handle this automatically with free certificates), and verify backups are running. Keep your old hosting for a week or two as a safety net before canceling — cheap insurance during the transition. Then enjoy the speed and stop worrying about server maintenance.

## What to watch for

The most common migration issues are DNS confusion (changes not propagating, or pointing to the wrong place), forgetting to move email if it was on the old host (managed WordPress hosts typically don't include email — arrange it separately), and canceling the old host too soon. None are serious if you plan for them. The migration itself, especially with a free professional service, is usually the easy part.

## The bottom line

Migrating to premium managed WordPress hosting is far less scary than it sounds, particularly when the host does it for you free. Back up first, use the free migration, test on staging, switch DNS during quiet hours, and keep the old host briefly as a safety net. The result is a faster, more reliable site with the tedious server work handled for you — and if you check for a current promo code before signing up, you'll pay less for the first year of it.
