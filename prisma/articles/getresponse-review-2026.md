GetResponse has been in email marketing since 1998 and has spent the last decade turning itself into an all-in-one marketing platform — email, landing pages, funnels, automation, and, unusually, webinars. That breadth is either the reason to buy it or the reason to skip it, depending on whether you'd actually use the extra rooms in the house. Here's an honest look.

## What it is now

Calling GetResponse an email tool undersells it and overselling it as a "marketing suite" oversells it. In practice it's a solid email platform with a genuinely useful automation builder, decent landing pages, and a few things nobody else bundles.

The company is Polish, which matters for one reason worth knowing: **it's EU-based, so GDPR compliance is native rather than bolted on**. For European businesses, that's a real consideration, and it's the kind of thing that only becomes obvious when a US-based competitor's data processing agreement causes a legal conversation.

## The genuinely distinctive part: webinars

No other mainstream email platform includes webinar hosting. If webinars are part of how you sell — courses, coaching, B2B demos — this consolidates two subscriptions into one and the integration is native: registrants flow into lists and automations without a Zapier hop.

If you'll never run a webinar, you're paying for a feature you won't touch, and you should weigh that honestly against a cheaper email-only tool.

## The automation builder is the real strength

GetResponse's visual workflow builder is one of the better ones in this price class. Conditions, filters, waits, scoring, and branching are all there and it's comprehensible without a training course. You can build genuine lifecycle automation — abandoned cart, onboarding sequences, re-engagement, tag-based segmentation — without moving to something like ActiveCampaign and its price tag.

This is where GetResponse beats simpler tools like AWeber or MailerLite decisively.

## Deliverability and the boring fundamentals

Deliverability is respectable — not the best in the industry, not a problem either. The list hygiene tools, suppression handling, and authentication setup (SPF/DKIM/DMARC guidance) are all present and adequately explained.

One honest note: deliverability is far more about **your** list quality and sending habits than your provider's reputation. People blame platforms for problems they created by buying a list or emailing people who forgot who they are. GetResponse won't save you from that, and neither will anyone else.

## Where it's genuinely weak

**The interface feels crowded.** All-in-one means a lot of navigation. Compared with the calm of ConvertKit or MailerLite, GetResponse is busy, and new users spend a while learning where things live.

**You pay for breadth you may not use.** If you only want to send a newsletter, you're subsidising webinars, funnels, and a landing page builder. MailerLite does the email-only job for less.

**Template design is dated in places.** The email builder works, but the stock templates look more 2019 than 2026. You'll want to build your own.

**Pricing scales with list size**, like everyone else, and the useful features (marketing automation, webinars) sit on higher tiers rather than the entry plan. Read the tier table before assuming the headline price is your price.

**Support is fine, not exceptional.** 24/7 chat, competent, occasionally scripted.

## Who it's actually for

**Small and mid-size businesses** that want email plus landing pages plus automation from one vendor and one bill, without ActiveCampaign's complexity or cost.

**Anyone running webinars** — this is the clearest case, and the consolidation genuinely saves money.

**European businesses** that value an EU-based processor.

## Who should skip it

**Creators and newsletter writers** who just want to write and send — ConvertKit or MailerLite are calmer, cheaper, and better at that specific job.

**Anyone at the very start with a tiny list** — the free tiers of MailerLite or AWeber will serve you fine until you have a reason to upgrade.

**Enterprises with complex CRM needs** — you'll outgrow it toward ActiveCampaign, HubSpot, or Klaviyo (if ecommerce).

## The honest verdict

GetResponse is a well-built, slightly unfashionable platform that does a lot of things at 80-90% quality and one thing (webinars) that nobody else does at all. It's rarely the *best* tool for any single job and frequently the *most sensible* tool when you need three jobs done from one vendor.

**Buy it if** you'd use at least two of email, automation, landing pages, and webinars. **Skip it if** you only want the first one.

Try the free plan with your real list before committing — the automation builder is the thing to test, because that's what you're actually paying for. And check for a current promotion first: annual billing saves meaningfully here, and email platforms discount often enough that paying list price on year one is avoidable.
