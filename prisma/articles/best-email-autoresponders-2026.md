An autoresponder is the least glamorous marketing tool and the one that quietly earns the most. It's the welcome sequence that turns a signup into a customer while you sleep, the onboarding series that stops churn, the re-engagement mail that wakes a dead list. Every email platform claims to do it. They're not equally good at it. Here's an honest comparison of the ones worth your money.

## What actually separates them

Ignore the feature grids. Three things decide whether an autoresponder works for you:

**Does mail arrive?** Deliverability beats every feature. A brilliant sequence in spam earns nothing.

**Can you express your actual logic?** "Send A, wait 3 days, send B" is table stakes. "If they clicked but didn't buy, branch here; if they're in segment B, skip step 3" separates the tools.

**What does it cost as the list grows?** Everyone's cheap at 500 subscribers. Look at 5,000 and 25,000.

## The honest shortlist

**GetResponse** — the best automation builder in its price class. Conditions, filters, waits, scoring, and real branching, built visually and comprehensibly. Also bundles webinars, which nobody else does, and landing pages that are a real product. EU-based, so GDPR is native. **Weakness:** crowded interface, dated stock templates, and the good features sit on higher tiers. **Best for:** small and mid-size businesses that need genuine lifecycle automation from one vendor.

**AWeber** — the veteran. Among the very best deliverability reputations in the industry, plain and simple to use, phone support at every tier including free, and an honest free plan up to 500 subscribers. **Weakness:** automation is basic — linear sequences and simple tag triggers; complex branching is awkward or absent. Pricing isn't competitive above the free tier. **Best for:** simple senders who value mail arriving and a human to call.

**MailerLite** — the value pick. Clean, modern, genuinely pleasant, with automation that's better than AWeber's and cheaper than almost everyone at every list size. **Weakness:** less depth than GetResponse at the complex end; approval process for new accounts can be picky. **Best for:** anyone optimising cost per capability.

**ConvertKit (Kit)** — built for creators. Tag-based rather than list-based (the right model), excellent for writers and course sellers, calm interface. **Weakness:** plain-text-forward design won't suit brands wanting rich templates; pricier at scale. **Best for:** newsletter writers, creators, course sellers.

**ActiveCampaign** — the ceiling. Best-in-class automation, CRM built in, conditional logic that handles anything you can describe. **Weakness:** expensive and genuinely complex — real onboarding time. **Best for:** businesses whose revenue depends on sophisticated lifecycle marketing.

**Klaviyo** — if you run a store, stop reading and use this. Ecommerce automation is a different discipline and Klaviyo owns it. Everything else on this list is second-best for that job.

## The sequence that matters more than the tool

Whichever you pick, the highest-return autoresponder is the same: **a welcome sequence**. Not a newsletter, not a campaign — the three-to-five emails that go out automatically after someone subscribes.

It works because it fires at the moment of maximum interest. Someone just chose to hear from you; that's the one moment they'll definitely open. Send: what they signed up for, then your best existing piece, then the thing you'd want them to know, then a soft ask.

Most people never build this. It takes an afternoon and outperforms months of broadcast emails.

## The traps

**Buying on features you won't use.** Ask what your *next twelve months* look like. If you'll send a weekly newsletter and one welcome sequence, ActiveCampaign is a waste. If you're building a course funnel, AWeber will block you.

**Ignoring the growth curve.** Compare at your projected list size, not today's. The ranking changes a lot between 500 and 25,000 subscribers.

**Blaming the platform for your list.** Deliverability is mostly *your* habits — how you collected addresses, how often you send, whether you clean. No provider saves a bought list. Set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC on day one and remove people who haven't opened in six months.

**Underestimating migration.** Switching means rebuilding automations, re-importing, re-warming, re-authenticating. Choose for two years out; you'd rather do this once.

## The honest verdict

**Start free.** MailerLite and AWeber both have genuine free tiers up to a few hundred subscribers — not countdown trials. Build your welcome sequence there and learn whether email works for your business before paying anyone.

**Then:** GetResponse if you need real automation or run webinars. MailerLite if you want the best value. ConvertKit if you're a creator. ActiveCampaign if automation *is* the strategy. Klaviyo if you sell products.

**AWeber** if simplicity and deliverability beat sophistication for you — a real position, not a consolation prize.

Whichever you choose, check for a current promotion before committing. Annual billing carries a meaningful discount across all of them, and email is one of the few subscriptions you'll genuinely still be paying for in five years — so the discount compounds.
