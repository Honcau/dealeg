A budget VPN is not the same as a bad VPN. The gap between the cheapest credible providers and the premium ones has narrowed to the point where, for most people's actual needs, paying $12/month is buying reassurance rather than capability. But the budget tier also contains genuinely bad options, and the difference matters. Here's how to spend little without buying something nasty.

## What "budget" gets you and what it doesn't

At roughly $2-3/month equivalent on a multi-year plan, you should expect: strong encryption, a working kill switch, apps on every platform, a decent server network, and a no-logs policy that has been audited by someone independent. That's the floor. Anything below that floor isn't a bargain — it's a different product wearing VPN clothes.

What you generally don't get at that price: top-tier speed, reliable unblocking of specific streaming libraries, and support that goes deep on technical problems.

## The honest shortlist

**Surfshark** — the strongest budget pick overall. Unlimited simultaneous devices (genuinely unlimited, not "10"), good speeds via WireGuard, reliable streaming, audited no-logs, and a Netherlands base. It's the one to beat, and it's usually only a little more than the true bottom tier. If you want one recommendation and no homework, this is it.

**PureVPN** — cheap, huge country list, lots of devices, and a serious audit programme. Its history includes the 2017 case where it handed connection logs to the FBI despite no-log marketing; it responded by relocating to the British Virgin Islands and commissioning repeated independent audits. Decent value if you've read that and are comfortable.

**Ivacy** — usually the cheapest credible option. Works, broad server list, rougher apps, middling speed. Shares corporate roots with PureVPN, so treat them as one family rather than two independent choices.

**Mullvad** — the wildcard. It's a flat €5/month with no discounts, no multi-year prepay, and no promotions ever, which makes it *look* expensive against a discounted three-year deal. But it never renews at a shock price, you can pay anonymously, and its privacy story is the cleanest in the industry. Over a full cycle it can be the cheaper honest choice.

## The trap that costs more than the VPN

**Renewal pricing.** Every provider on this list except Mullvad advertises a multi-year prepay rate and renews far higher — often two to four times. People buy a "$1.99/month" plan and then quietly pay triple from year three onward.

Two defences: set a calendar reminder a week before renewal, and treat the prepay term as the real commitment. If a three-year plan at $2 renews at $8, your honest five-year average isn't $2.

## What to check before buying anything cheap

- **Independent audit.** Has a real firm inspected the no-logs claim? Recently? A blog post isn't an audit.
- **Jurisdiction.** Where is the company based, and does that country compel data retention?
- **Kill switch that works.** Test it: connect, start a download, kill the VPN process, confirm traffic stops.
- **What's an add-on.** Port forwarding, dedicated IP, and split tunnelling are extras at several budget providers. Price your actual configuration.
- **Refund window.** Most offer 30 days. Use it — test speed on your real connection in week one.

## What a cheap VPN cannot do

Worth saying plainly, because a lot of marketing implies otherwise. A VPN hides your traffic from your ISP and from the network you're on, and changes your apparent location. It does not make you anonymous, it does not stop websites profiling you through your logged-in accounts and browser fingerprint, and it does not protect a device that's already compromised.

If your threat model is "I don't want the café Wi-Fi or my ISP seeing what I browse," a budget VPN solves it completely. If your threat model involves a state actor, no consumer VPN at any price is your answer.

## The honest verdict

**Buy Surfshark** if you want the best budget VPN and can live with "cheap but not cheapest." Unlimited devices plus real speed makes it the sensible default.

**Buy PureVPN or Ivacy** if price is genuinely the binding constraint and you've read the history notes above.

**Buy Mullvad** if you want the honest flat price and the cleanest privacy story, and you'd rather never think about renewals again.

**Don't buy** anything advertising a "lifetime" VPN deal, anything with no audit, or anything free. The economics of running a VPN network mean somebody pays — and if it isn't you, it's your data.

Whichever you pick, buy on a promotion, test it inside the refund window, and diarise the renewal date. That single calendar reminder is worth more than the difference between any two providers on this list.
