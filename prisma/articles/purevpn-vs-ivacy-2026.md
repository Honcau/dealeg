This is an unusual comparison, because the honest answer starts with something most articles skip: PureVPN and Ivacy are widely reported to share corporate roots — the same parent group operates both brands. So this isn't two rivals fighting it out. It's closer to comparing two products from the same house, aimed at slightly different buyers.

That reframes everything, so let's start there.

## Why the shared ownership matters

If you're choosing between them purely on features and price, it doesn't matter much — pick whichever is cheaper for the configuration you want.

It matters a lot if your reason for comparing is **trust diversification**. Picking Ivacy because you're uneasy about PureVPN's 2017 logging incident — where PureVPN provided connection logs to the FBI despite marketing itself as no-log — doesn't get you as far from that history as it appears. Same corporate family, same broad governance.

It also matters for redundancy. Some people run two VPN subscriptions so that if one provider is blocked, compromised, or shut down, they have a fallback. Two brands under one roof is weak redundancy.

## Where they actually differ

**Positioning.** PureVPN is the flagship: more marketing, more features, more visible audit activity, and the brand that gets reviewed. Ivacy is the value line — cheaper, quieter, less polished.

**Price.** Both are cheap on multi-year plans; Ivacy is usually the cheaper of the two at deep discount. Neither is cheap at renewal.

**Audits and transparency.** PureVPN has done the more visible work here — repeated independent no-logs audits, including ongoing verification arrangements, plus a move to a British Virgin Islands base. Ivacy has audit history too, but PureVPN is the one that has publicly leaned into it, largely because it's the one that had to.

**Apps and polish.** PureVPN's clients are somewhat more refined and updated more visibly. Ivacy's work but feel a step behind.

**Speed.** Broadly similar — both are adequate rather than fast. Neither belongs in a conversation with the top tier.

**Extras.** Both sell port forwarding, dedicated IP, and similar features as **paid add-ons** rather than including them. Compare the price of your actual configuration, not the headline.

## The shared weaknesses

Because they're built from similar foundations, they share a weakness list:

- Speed is middling — noticeable on long-distance connections and large transfers
- Streaming unblocking is inconsistent and shouldn't be your reason to buy either
- Add-on pricing means the advertised rate often isn't your real rate
- Renewal pricing is dramatically higher than the promo rate on both

## So which one?

If you've decided this family is where you're shopping, the tiebreak is simple:

**Pick PureVPN if** you want the more polished apps, the more visible audit trail, and the broader feature set — and you accept the 2017 history in light of the years of remediation since.

**Pick Ivacy if** the only thing you care about is the lowest number, and you're fine with slightly rougher software for it.

Honestly, at typical discounted prices the gap between them is small enough that whichever is cheaper this month is a defensible answer.

## The better question

The more useful question isn't PureVPN vs Ivacy — it's whether either is the right buy at all.

**If price is genuinely the binding constraint**, yes. Both work, both encrypt your traffic, both cover a lot of devices for very little money. That's a real value proposition and there's no shame in it.

**If you have another $1-2/month of room**, Surfshark sits just above with better speed, unlimited devices, and reliable streaming. NordVPN costs a bit more again and is meaningfully faster. Mullvad costs a flat rate with no promotions but has the cleanest trust story in the industry.

Most people comparing PureVPN and Ivacy are optimising in the wrong dimension — squeezing the last dollar out of the budget tier when a small step up buys a noticeably better product.

## The honest verdict

They're two brands from the same house, priced slightly differently, performing similarly. Pick whichever is cheaper if you're set on this tier; understand that choosing one over the other doesn't buy you distance from the other's history. And before you commit to a three-year prepay for the discount, ask whether a slightly better VPN is worth a dollar more a month. For most people, it is.
