PureVPN has been around since 2007, which in VPN years makes it ancient. It's cheap on long plans, has a huge server network, and shows up on every "best VPN" list that ranks by price. It also carries the most-cited trust incident in the industry. Both of those things are true at once, and you can't evaluate PureVPN honestly without holding both. Here's the full picture.

## Let's start with the elephant

In 2017, PureVPN provided logs to the FBI in a criminal stalking case, despite marketing itself as a no-log provider. The logs were connection metadata rather than browsing content, and the case involved genuinely serious conduct — but it directly contradicted the company's own claims at the time. That is a real event, not a rumour, and anyone recommending PureVPN without mentioning it isn't being straight with you.

What PureVPN did afterwards matters too. The company moved its base to the British Virgin Islands (outside mandatory data-retention regimes), rewrote its logging policy, and has since commissioned repeated independent audits of its no-logs claims, including always-on style verification arrangements. Those audits are real and more frequent than many competitors bother with.

So: an old, bad incident, followed by nearly a decade of visible remediation. Whether that's redemption or a permanent asterisk is a judgement call — but it should be **your** judgement, made with the facts.

## What's good about it

**Price.** PureVPN's multi-year plans are aggressively cheap, often landing near the bottom of the market per month. If budget is the binding constraint, it competes hard.

**Server network.** Thousands of servers across a very wide country list, including places smaller providers skip. If you need an exit in an unusual country, PureVPN usually has one.

**Device coverage.** Apps for everything, generous simultaneous connections, router support, and browser extensions. One subscription genuinely covers a household.

**Extras.** Port forwarding, dedicated IP, and split tunnelling are available, several as paid add-ons rather than included.

## What's not

**Speed is fine, not exceptional.** It's adequate for streaming and general browsing; it isn't in the top tier that Mullvad, ExpressVPN, or NordVPN occupy on the fastest protocols. You'll notice on long-distance connections.

**The upsell.** Several useful features are add-ons at checkout rather than parts of the plan. The advertised headline price isn't always the price of the configuration you want.

**Renewal pricing.** Like most VPNs, the cheap number is a multi-year introductory rate. Renewal is meaningfully higher. Budget for the real number or diarise the cancellation.

**Streaming is hit and miss.** It works with major services often enough, but if unblocking a specific library is your main reason for buying, PureVPN is less reliable than the providers that specialise in it.

## Who should actually buy it

PureVPN makes sense if you want a competent, cheap VPN for **everyday privacy hygiene** — public Wi-Fi, hiding traffic from your ISP, casual geo-shifting — and you're comfortable with the company's history given the subsequent audits.

It does not make sense if your threat model is serious. If you're a journalist, activist, or anyone whose safety depends on the provider never logging anything, the 2017 incident is exactly the kind of event that should push you toward a provider with a cleaner record and a stronger structural argument — Mullvad's anonymous-account model, for instance.

## The honest verdict

**Choose PureVPN if** price is your main constraint, you want wide country coverage and lots of devices, and you've read the history and accept it. On those terms it's decent value and the audit trail since is genuinely better than average.

**Choose something else if** you need top-tier speed, reliable streaming unblocking, or a provider whose no-logs claim has never been tested and found wanting. NordVPN and Surfshark cost a little more and give you both.

The fair summary: PureVPN is a cheap, capable, well-audited VPN with a bad thing in its past that it has spent years addressing. That's not disqualifying for most people's actual needs, and it's absolutely disqualifying for a few. Know which group you're in before you buy — and if you do buy, buy on a promotion and set a reminder before renewal.
