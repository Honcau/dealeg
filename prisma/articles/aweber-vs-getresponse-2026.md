AWeber and GetResponse both launched in 1998, both survived every shake-up in email marketing since, and both get recommended constantly. But they've aged in opposite directions: AWeber stayed simple and doubled down on fundamentals; GetResponse kept adding rooms until it became a marketing suite. Neither choice is wrong. Here's how to tell which one is wrong *for you*.

## The one-line difference

**AWeber does email well and stops.** **GetResponse does email, automation, landing pages, funnels, and webinars, at slightly less depth each.**

Everything below follows from that.

## Automation: not a close contest

This is the deciding factor for most people, so let's not bury it.

**GetResponse wins decisively.** Its visual workflow builder handles conditions, filters, waits, scoring, and real branching — proper lifecycle automation like abandoned cart, tag-based onboarding, and re-engagement, built visually and comprehensibly.

**AWeber's Campaigns builder is basic.** Linear sequences and simple tag triggers, competently done. Anything resembling "if they clicked X but didn't buy within 5 days and are in segment B" is awkward or impossible.

If automation is central to your marketing, the comparison ends here and GetResponse wins.

## Where AWeber wins

**Deliverability reputation.** Twenty-plus years of sender reputation, and it shows. Both are good; AWeber is among the very best. Your email arriving beats every feature on the list.

**Simplicity.** Lists, messages, campaigns. No six-hour onboarding, no navigating a suite. If you want to write and send, AWeber gets out of the way in a way GetResponse has forgotten how to.

**Phone support at every tier**, including free. GetResponse is chat-first. For non-technical business owners this is a genuine differentiator.

**The free tier.** Up to 500 subscribers with real sending, landing pages, and basic automation — a working plan, not a countdown trial.

## Where GetResponse wins

**Webinars.** Nobody else in mainstream email bundles webinar hosting. If webinars are part of how you sell — courses, coaching, B2B demos — this consolidates two subscriptions, and registrants flow into automations natively with no Zapier hop. This alone justifies GetResponse for a specific audience.

**Landing pages and funnels** are a real product, not an afterthought.

**EU-based (Poland)**, so GDPR handling is native rather than bolted on. For European businesses that's a real consideration.

**Value per dollar at mid-size.** Once you're paying anyway, GetResponse gives you far more capability for similar money.

## Where each genuinely hurts

**AWeber:** dated interface and templates, shallow segmentation, weak automation, and pricing that isn't competitive above the free tier — MailerLite generally does more for less.

**GetResponse:** crowded interface (all-in-one means a lot of navigation), you subsidise features you may never use, stock templates look a few years behind, and the good stuff (automation, webinars) sits on higher tiers rather than the entry plan. Read the tier table before assuming the headline price applies to you.

## The decision, simplified

**Choose AWeber if:** you're under 500 subscribers and want an honest free plan; you send a straightforward newsletter and value it arriving; you want to phone a human; or simplicity genuinely matters more to you than capability.

**Choose GetResponse if:** you need real automation; you run webinars; you want landing pages and email from one vendor; or you're EU-based and value that.

**Choose neither if:** you're a creator or newsletter writer (ConvertKit or MailerLite are calmer, cheaper, better at that job), you run a store (Klaviyo), or you need best-in-class automation and will pay for it (ActiveCampaign).

## The migration angle nobody mentions

Switching email platforms is genuinely painful — you rebuild automations, re-import lists, re-warm deliverability, and re-authenticate your domain. You'd rather do it once.

So don't choose for where you are; choose for where you'll be in two years. If your marketing will get more sophisticated, starting on AWeber means a migration you can see coming. If it genuinely won't — you send a newsletter, that's the plan, that's always been the plan — then GetResponse's extra rooms are just rent.

## The honest verdict

**GetResponse is the better platform** for most businesses buying today. The automation gap is large, the webinar bundle is unique, and once you're paying anyway you get considerably more.

**AWeber is the better tool** for a narrow, real segment: simple senders under 500 subscribers on the free plan, and people who want deliverability and a phone number rather than a workflow canvas.

Test both free before committing — AWeber's free tier and GetResponse's trial both let you send to your real list, and the automation builder is the thing to actually try. Then check for a current promotion: both discount annual billing meaningfully, and email is a subscription you'll keep for years.
